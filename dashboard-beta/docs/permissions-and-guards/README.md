# Permissions and guards

What a person may do, what a machine may do, and where the line is drawn —
and why each refusal says what it says.

The shape, in one paragraph: purple is a person's press. A button that is
purple, a field with a purple outline, a setting listed as "at the window" —
none of them can be reached from the command line, from a machine, or from a
driven window, and the refusal names the reason. Everything else is one
surface: the window, the command line and the next person use the same
actions with the same rules.

Pages in this suite:

- what the supervisor may call (`allowed.js`) and why the rest is withheld
- the window-only settings (drills, trust, speaking in your name)
- what a judge may not do to what it reads
- what goes out under your name, and the draft step in front of it
- the guards list at Settings → Guards, which is the complete answer
